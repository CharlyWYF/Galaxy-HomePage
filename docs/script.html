Slide 2 - Why Customer Churn Matters

Charly

First, we need to explain why customer churn prediction is worth studying.
The key impact of churn is that it not only reduces current revenue, but also increases the future cost of acquiring new customers.
In general, retaining existing customers is cheaper than acquiring new ones. Therefore, if a company can identify customers who are likely to leave in advance, it can take earlier actions such as offering discounts, improving service, or providing more personalized communication.
For this reason, customer churn prediction has clear business value and strong data analysis significance.

Slide 3 - Application Areas

宋

Next, we can look at where this problem can be applied.
Customer churn prediction is not limited to a single industry.
It can be used in telecommunications, for example to predict whether users may cancel services or switch providers. It can also be used by subscription platforms to predict whether members may stop renewing. In e-commerce, it can help identify users who may stop purchasing. In banking and financial services, it can predict whether customers may close accounts or stop using certain products.
These industries share one common feature: customers can switch relatively easily, so early identification of high-risk customers is very valuable.

Slide 4 - Problem Definition

宋

After clarifying the application scenarios, we can define the problem more precisely.
This project treats customer churn prediction as a binary classification problem.
In other words, the model needs to use historical customer data to predict whether a customer will churn or not churn.
The input data mainly comes from customer profiles, billing information, and service usage behavior. The output can help companies decide which customers should be prioritized for attention and intervention.
Therefore, this is not only a prediction problem, but also a problem of supporting business decision-making.

Slide 5 - Data Source

健

After defining the problem, the next key question is where the data comes from.
The public data source we propose is the Telco Customer Churn dataset on Kaggle.
This dataset is suitable for this assignment because it contains customer profile information, billing information, service usage details, and the most important churn label.
It is publicly available, clearly structured, and appropriate for exploratory data analysis and classification modelling.
Therefore, it can meet the requirements of Part 1 and also provide a good foundation for model implementation in Part 2.

Slide 6 - Data Structure

健

Next, we explain what types of data are usually needed for customer churn prediction.
We divide the required data into four groups.
The first group is customer profile, such as gender, whether the customer is a senior citizen, and whether the customer has a partner or dependents.
The second group is account information, including contract type, payment method, tenure, monthly charges, and total charges.
The third group is service usage, such as whether the customer uses internet service, phone service, technical support, or streaming services.
The final group is the target label, which indicates churn or no churn.
The key point is that customer churn is usually not caused by a single factor, but by the combination of multiple factors.

Slide 7 - EDA Plan

飞

Before formal modelling, we need to conduct exploratory data analysis, or EDA.
EDA has two main purposes: first, to check data quality, and second, to discover potential patterns.
Specifically, we will first check missing values, duplicate records, and whether the data types are correct.
Then, we will examine the distribution of churn and non-churn customers to see whether the classes are balanced.
Next, we will focus on key variables such as tenure, monthly charges, and total charges, and examine whether they have clear relationships with churn.
We will also compare churn patterns across different contract types and payment methods.
Through this analysis, we can better understand which variables may be important drivers of customer churn.

Slide 8 - Proposed AI Methods

飞

After understanding the data, we need to choose suitable methods.
Based on the characteristics of this problem, we recommend three classical machine learning methods: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree, and Random Forest.
First, Logistic Regression is suitable as a baseline model because it is simple, efficient, and highly interpretable.
Second, the advantage of Decision Tree is that it can present classification logic clearly, so it is also easy to explain in a classroom presentation.
Finally, Random Forest is an ensemble method. It is usually more robust than a single decision tree and can better handle complex non-linear relationships.
Therefore, these three methods provide a good balance between interpretability and predictive performance.

Slide 9 - Evaluation Metrics

洲

After building the models, we also need to define how to evaluate them.
The metrics we plan to use include accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and the confusion matrix.
One point that needs special emphasis is that in customer churn prediction, recall is often more important than accuracy alone.
If the model fails to identify customers who are truly at risk of churn, the company loses the opportunity to intervene early, which may lead to real business loss.
Therefore, when evaluating the model, we should not only look at overall accuracy, but also consider the business meaning of different types of errors.
